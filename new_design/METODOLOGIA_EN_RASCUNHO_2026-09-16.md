# Methodology

## 1. A Declared Yardstick

Every analysis of cultural content measures the work against some yardstick — almost always implicit, rarely confessed. Philosify does the opposite: the yardstick is in plain view. Each analysis evaluates music, film, literature, news and ideas from an explicit philosophical framework, Objectivism, and says so in the first line.

You do not need to agree with the yardstick to use Philosify. You need to know what it is — and here it is, with the argument for why we chose it. A declared premise is a premise that can be examined. That is exactly what we ask of the content we analyze; it would be incoherent not to do the same with our own starting point.

## 2. Why Objectivism

*By Bob Rach, founder of Philosify.*

The strongest reason to adopt Objectivism is not "because Ayn Rand is persuasive," nor because its political results look desirable. It is the reverse: because, by starting from what is most basic and impossible to deny, it offers an integrated system for turning reality into knowledge, knowledge into values, and values into action.

The starting point is simple: reality exists independently of what we wish were true. Existence exists. Things have identity. And what exists acts according to what it is. It sounds trivial, but it eliminates an enormous amount of confusion: reality is not a product of consciousness, of language, of culture, of political will, of consensus or of faith. Consciousness does not create what it perceives; its function is to identify what exists.

From this follows the second fundamental choice: if we want to know reality, we must use reason. Not because reason is infallible — human beings err — but because we have no other instrument capable of correcting its own errors. Perception supplies the material; concepts organize that material; logic checks the integration; evidence confronts our conclusions with the facts.

That is why Objectivism can be regarded not merely as a philosophy, but as an intellectual operating system for living on Earth:

- It begins with reality, not with a narrative. It asks first "what exists?", then "how do we know?", and only then "what should we do?". This keeps moral or political wishes from manufacturing convenient facts.
- It establishes a hierarchy of knowledge. An economic theory cannot contradict human nature; an ethics cannot contradict reality; a politics cannot destroy what makes human life possible.
- It demands integration and the absence of contradictions. When two conclusions collide, some premise is wrong, incomplete or out of context. Thinking becomes a permanent process of integration.
- It treats concepts as instruments tied to reality. "Justice," "liberty," "equality," "rights," "self-interest" cannot be sounds charged with emotion. They must have objective meaning and referents in the world.
- It recognizes context. A rational conclusion is the one supported by all the available evidence within the known context. This allows certainty without demanding omniscience.
- It provides an objective standard of value: human life. Not momentary survival, but a properly human life, sustained by reason.
- It turns virtues into practical requirements. Rationality, productiveness, independence, integrity, honesty, justice and pride are not supernatural commandments; they are the principles anyone needs in order to live as a rational being.
- It dissolves the false opposition between self-interest and morality. The individual does not have to choose between sacrificing others and self-sacrifice to others. One can rationally pursue one's own happiness through production, creation, friendship, love and voluntary trade.
- It gives art a cognitive place, not a decorative one. Art is a selective re-creation of reality according to the artist's most fundamental value judgments: what the artist chooses to show — and how — reveals what the artist considers important, possible and worthy in man and in the world. That is why a song, a film or a novel carries a philosophy even when it does not declare one, and that is why it can be read. This is the foundation of what Philosify does.
- It derives a politics of individual rights. If every person must exercise their own judgment in order to live, no one can morally acquire the right to replace that judgment with force.

The greatest merit of Objectivism as a method is this: it forces the prior question. Before "which economic policy should we adopt?", it asks "what is man?". Before "what is it fair to distribute?", it asks "who produced it?". Before "which rights should we create?", it asks "what is a right?". And before all of these: "is it true?"

One important consequence: no one should elect Objectivism and then go looking for arguments to defend it. That would be profoundly anti-Objectivist. The correct procedure is reality → observation → concepts → principles → integration → conclusion — and then to check whether Objectivism survives that process better than its competitors. If it does, it deserves to be adopted not as a belief, but as the best available identification of reality and of the proper way to live in it.

In one sentence: Objectivism deserves to be the philosophy for living on Earth because it begins by asking what reality demands of man, instead of asking what man would like to demand of reality.

## 3. How Philosify Evaluates

Each analysis examines the work along five axes, the classical branches of philosophy, with weights that reflect how much each one bears on the message of a cultural work:

| Axis | Weight | What it examines |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ethics | 40% | Which standard of value the work defends or presupposes; what it presents as good and as evil |
| Metaphysics | 20% | How the work treats reality: as something to be known and faced, or as malleable to will, wish or chance |
| Epistemology | 20% | How truth is reached in the work: through reason and evidence, or through faith, emotion, authority |
| Politics | 10% | How the work conceives relations between people: voluntary trade and individual rights, or force and the collective |
| Aesthetics | 10% | What view of man and of the world the work's form projects |

Each axis receives a score from −10 to +10 — negative when the work contradicts the principles of the framework, positive when it affirms them. The weighted score produces the work's verdict, on a scale that runs from Doctrinaire Conformist to Extremely Revolutionary.

The names on the scale do not measure artistic quality or formal originality. They measure the work's relation to the culture's dominant premises. The prevailing culture — in ethics, metaphysics, epistemology, politics and aesthetics — is, to a large extent, the opposite of the Objectivist framework: it treats reality as negotiable, reason as one voice among others, sacrifice as a virtue, the collective as the source of rights, and form as the expression of subjective states. A work that repeats these premises is not "wrong only in relation to us"; it is aligned with the ambient doctrine. That is why the negative pole is called conformist: the work confirms what already circulates as obvious.

The positive pole is called revolutionary for the symmetrical reason. To affirm that reality is independent of consciousness, that reason is the only means of knowledge, that the individual's life is the standard of value and that force creates no rights is not the common sense of the age. It is a break with the philosophy the culture already teaches — at school, in art, in the news and in habit. Revolutionary, here, does not mean rupture for its own sake, nor aesthetic avant-garde. It means that the work sustains premises which, taken seriously, require reorganizing the prevailing way of judging the real, the true, the good, the just and the beautiful.

The word "doctrinaire," on the conformist pole, marks systematic adherence to that ambient doctrine, not an isolated slip. "Extremely," on the revolutionary pole, marks consistent integration of Objectivist premises, not an isolated gesture of nonconformity. Between the two poles there are degrees: a work can be partly conformist on one axis and partly revolutionary on another. The weighted verdict sums up that tension; it does not erase it.

Nor is the verdict a grade for artistic quality: a masterpiece can receive −8 and a mediocre work +6. What is measured is the philosophy the work carries, not the talent with which it carries it.

A few operating principles:

- Works with several voices are judged by the message of the whole, not by the behavior of isolated characters. A well-written villain does not make the work villainous.
- The same work always receives the same scores, in every language. Scores are computed once and preserved; only the explanatory text is translated. Consistency is a condition of honesty: a yardstick that changes with the language is not a yardstick.
- Every analysis carries a SHA-256 signature, which lets anyone verify that the text being shared is the original text, unaltered.
- The Philosopher Panel gathers 266 thinkers from 2,600 years of history, each with documented positions on the five axes, and simulates how each of them would examine the work from their own premises. It is a tool of contrast: it shows that the Objectivist yardstick is one among many — and why we chose this one.
- Analyses are generated with the help of AI models from more than one provider, under instructions that fix the framework, the axes and the scale. The AI applies the method; the method is ours.

## 4. What an Analysis Is — and What It Is Not

A Philosify analysis is a reasoned philosophical reading: it identifies a work's premises and confronts them with a declared framework. It is grounded opinion, not fact; interpretation, not ruling.

It is not a judgment on the work's artistic quality; not a consumption recommendation; not a claim about the author's intentions; nor a final truth. You can — and should — disagree with it. The yardstick is in plain view precisely so that disagreement is possible: whoever knows the premise can contest the conclusion with precision, instead of reacting to a verdict of unknown origin.

## 5. How to Read an Analysis

Start with the verdict and the five scores: they tell you where the work and the framework agree or collide. Then read the argument for each axis: it tells you why. If some axis seems wrong, ask yourself which premise you reject — the work's, the framework's or the analysis's. That exercise is the product. The scores are only the beginning of the conversation.

## ESCOLHAS DE TRADUÇÃO QUE PEDEM O SEU OLHO

1. **"régua" → "yardstick"** (título "A Declared Yardstick"; "the yardstick is in plain view"; "a yardstick that changes
   with the language is not a yardstick"). Alternativas: "ruler" (literal, mas em EN lê como "governante"), "standard"
   (perde a imagem de medição). Yardstick mantém a metáfora e a repetição do PT.
2. **Polo conformista = "Doctrinaire Conformist"** (UI-EN). O PT argumenta sobre o *prefixo* "doutrinariamente"; em EN
   o rótulo é adjetivo, então a frase virou *"The word 'doctrinaire,' on the conformist pole, marks systematic
   adherence…"*. Mesmo argumento, sem inventar um advérbio que a UI não mostra. (Espelho da decisão 1 de 01/09:
   em PT a UI é que muda para "Doutrinariamente Conformista"; em EN nada muda.)
3. **"o homem" → "man"** onde o PT usa o termo como conceito ("what is man?", "in man and in the world", "what reality
   demands of man"). É o registro randiano e o eco do PT. Nos itens sobre o indivíduo genérico usei "anyone", "one",
   "the artist", "their" — sem "he" — para não datar o texto onde o PT não obriga.
4. **"egoísmo" → "self-interest"** (item 4 da lista, entre aspas, e item 8 "the false opposition between self-interest
   and morality"). Segue a regra do projeto ("virtuous self-interest", nunca "rational egoism"). "Selfishness" seria
   mais rand-literal e mais provocador; fica a seu critério.
5. **"sistema operacional intelectual para viver na Terra" → "an intellectual operating system for living on Earth"**,
   e a frase final da §2 fecha com **"the philosophy for living on Earth"** — eco deliberado de Rand ("a philosophy
   for living on earth"). Mantive "Earth" maiúsculo como no PT.
6. **"sentença" (§4) → "ruling"** ("interpretation, not ruling"). "Sentence" em EN lê como frase gramatical; "verdict"
   colidiria com o termo técnico do produto.
7. **"doutrina ambiente" → "the ambient doctrine"**; "cultura vigente" → "the prevailing culture"; "senso comum da
   época" → "the common sense of the age".
8. **"é possível lê-la" (a filosofia da obra) → "that is why it can be read"** — o "it" retoma a philosophy, como o
   "la" do PT.
9. Número do Painel: **266 thinkers / 2,600 years**, igual ao canônico. Se o total mudou desde 01/09, é só trocar aqui
   e no PT ao mesmo tempo.
10. Setas do procedimento (reality → observation → …) mantidas como caracteres, iguais ao PT; no HTML final entram como
